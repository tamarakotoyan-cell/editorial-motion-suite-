# 08 — Layered editorial (Structure C)

The register between A and B. One cut subject, flat glyph satellites, and a worked field
held inside a window — or a second treated mass that seats the subject. It is how the
reference set gets collage energy without collage assets, and it is buildable with **one**
photographic cutout, which dissolves most of the Structure B asset problem rather than
solving it.

Derived from the Canva-assembled algorithm tile (19 Aug 2026), read against
`04-graphics-imagery`, `07-focal-point` and the reference set, then proved in HTML as
the 19 Aug 2026 proof tile (albo-75-tile.html, pilot folder) — the ground-mass build. Routing between A, B and C is
in `04-graphics-imagery`; this file is the build grammar once C has been chosen.

Paragraphs marked **[proposal]** are rules this register needs that the master did not
already carry. They are stated so they can be accepted, edited or struck; nothing else in
the master was loosened to make room for them.

---

## What this register is, in the system's own vocabulary

Most of it is not new machinery. The register works by combining three devices the system
already sanctions, and the finding is that **combined, they are a repeatable structure**:

- **The foreign-register exception** (`04`): exactly one photographic element among flat
  vector ones, and it is the thing the frame is about. The subject is the only photograph
  on the canvas — that difference is the focal point (`07`'s register carrier).
- **A shape as a window** (`04`): one silhouette or object form holding the dense
  material. Density lives inside the window; the field outside stays calm.
- **The worked surface** (`03`): the ground is paper, not pixels — and the loud texture is
  quarantined inside the window, not sprayed across the frame.

Where the claim "nothing new" **does not hold**, and it is said here rather than hidden:

1. **The ground-mass variant carries two photographic assets.** `04`'s exception is
   arithmetic — *exactly one element* — and the Parliament tile has a subject and a mass.
   The rule below re-reads "one photographic *element*" as "one photographic *subject*",
   with tests that keep the mass from becoming a second subject. That is a relaxation of
   `04` and it is a **[proposal]**. If it is refused, the ground-mass variant is out and
   the proof tile is rebuilt as a window build.
2. **Texture zoning re-reads "one texture layer".** `03` and `brand.md` say one texture
   layer, grain at 4–6%. This register runs paper tooth on the ground, a loud field inside
   the window and a dot screen on the subject — three textures, each in its own zone. The
   reading is *one texture layer per zone, and the ground itself still carries exactly
   one*. Also a **[proposal]**.
3. **Glyph satellites sit close to the icon ban.** `04` bans pictograms standing in for
   concepts, and `06-F` bans concept icon sets. The satellites here are objects in the
   scene, not labels for ideas — the distinction `imagery-motion` already draws for the
   motion side (*icons banned as data marks; fine as props*). The carve-out and its tests
   are below, as a **[proposal]**. Without it, C is banned by the ban list's own
   precedence, and the ban list wins.

## Preconditions — decidable before design starts

| Needs | Count |
|---|---|
| Silhouette-cut photographic subject (halftoned) | **exactly 1** |
| Flat single-colour glyph silhouettes | 3–6 |
| *Either* a window shape (an object form the story owns) with a worked field inside it | 1 |
| *Or* a second treated mass that seats the subject (the ground-mass variant) | 1 |

One cutout. Not ten. If the subject cannot be cut on its silhouette, build A. If someone
reaches for a second photograph *as a subject*, they are building B and the ten-element
precondition applies in full.

## Two builds

**The window build** — a loud field inside an object shape. The CRT tile: static and
moiré held inside a screen shape, the subject cropped by the window's edge, satellites
breaking the edge from both sides. The window is what quarantines the density.

**The ground-mass build** — no window. A second treated mass (a skyline, a building, a
horizon, a floor) sits in front of the subject's foot and *seats* it: it ends the subject
so the cutout does not simply stop, and it does the window's job of anchoring the
composition to a place. The proof tile: the halftoned PM behind a duotoned Parliament House
that bleeds both side edges. In this build the loud texture **is the subject's own
halftone** — nothing else on the frame is screened.

**[proposal] The mass is ground, not subject.** It may exist only if all of these hold:

- one mass, never two;
- it goes through the same pipeline and the same duotone map as the subject, but is
  **duotoned smooth, never halftoned** — the subject's dot screen must remain the only
  screened thing on the frame, or the register carrier is gone;
- it breaks at least two frame edges — it is a mass, not a figure, and a mass has no
  outline the eye can trace all the way round;
- it carries no face and no figure — nothing the eye could take for a second subject;
- it sits in front of the subject's foot or behind the subject's whole body. It seats or it
  backs; it never sits at the subject's own depth beside it.

Fail one and it is a second photograph, `04`'s exception no longer applies, and the frame
is a two-cutout pinboard.

## The layer grammar — seven layers, in order

1. **Ground** — paper, light, creased or toothed, near-neutral. Carries nothing else.
2. **Window shape** — one object form, hard-edged, filled by layer 3. *(Ground-mass build:
   this layer and the next are replaced by the mass, which sits at layer 5½ — see below.)*
3. **Field texture** — the loud layer: static, moiré, halftone wash, dense pattern. Exists
   *only* inside the window.
4. **Back satellites** — 1–2 glyphs or illustrated objects, partly behind the subject. A
   ghosted artefact (`04`) may sit here too — the proof tile runs a giant secondary-value
   `75` as the back plane.
5. **Subject** — the halftoned cutout. Cropped by the window's edge, never floating clear
   of it. *(Ground-mass build: the mass sits immediately in front of the subject here,
   occluding its foot.)*
6. **Front satellites** — 1–2 glyphs overlapping the subject or the window edge.
7. **Type block** — on the clean ground, never inside the window.

**The subject is overlapped from both sides** — something behind it, something in front of
it. That is what seats it in a scene rather than stickering it on. A subject nothing
touches reads as pasted, however good the cutout.

**[proposal] Scale ladder:** largest satellite ≥3× the smallest; no two satellites within
20% of each other's size. Uniform satellites are the sticker-sheet failure from `04`.

## Texture is zoned, never stacked

Three textures may coexist because each owns a zone and the zones do not overlap:

| Zone | Texture | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Ground | paper tooth / crease | subtle — reads as stock, not as weather |
| Inside the window | the loud field | as dense as it likes |
| Subject | halftone dot | the subject's own, from the pipeline |
| Ground mass (if any) | smooth duotone | no screen — see above |
| Glyphs and type | **none — flat and clean** | absolute |

The glyphs' flatness is load-bearing: they are the calm that makes the window read as loud
and the photograph read as photographic. Texture the glyphs and the register contrast
collapses. The type block sits on clean ground — the busy field is quarantined behind the
window edge, which is why this structure never has a type-legibility problem. Type never
enters the window.

## Satellites are objects, not icons

**[proposal]** A satellite is a thing in the scene the subject is in — the phone, the
bell, the bubble, the ballot box, the microphone. It earns its place the way a prop does,
by being handled: overlapped, occluded, scaled against its neighbours. It fails into the
icon ban the moment it behaves like a label:

- a satellite that could take a caption is an icon;
- satellites in a row, a ring or a grid are an icon set;
- one satellite per concept in the copy is a pictogram list;
- a satellite at the same scale as its neighbours is a sticker.

Three to six, all from one vocabulary, all in one ink, at wildly different sizes, touching
the subject or the window edge. `04`'s icon ban and `06-F` still apply in full to
everything that is not a satellite by these tests.

## Palette under density

The field texture may be polychrome (CRT static, print moiré) **only if it reads as a
single mid value at 200px wide** — colour as dither, not as palette. Test by downscaling:
if the field reads as "colourful" rather than "grey-ish", it has become a second palette
and breaks the accent rule.

Everything else: glyphs in one ink. Subject duotoned or halftoned to the composition's map
(highlight stays a colour — `04`'s lightest-pixel test applies). Ground near-neutral.
**Accent on exactly one element class**, as ever. Where the duotone map's highlight is the
brand accent, the treated assets are that class, and the round full stop pairs with it the
way the worked example pairs its accent block and dot — one class plus the mandated stop.

## Type on this register

The figure is the headline. On a stat-led layered tile there is no separate display
sentence: the hero figure *is* the display, and the sentence beside it is support at
roughly a third of the figure's height. (This is `02`'s register-clustering read — the
figure is the display, run large — not a new rule; but it does mean `02`'s 1.2–2× hero
ratio is measured against nothing on this frame, and `02` should say so if the reading is
accepted.) Round accent full stop closes the sentence. Source and base at the foot, above
the safe line — non-negotiable, and missing from the Canva source tile.

## Ban list for this register

The house ban list (`06-anti-patterns`) wins over everything here. In addition:

- ⛔ A second photograph *as a subject*. One is a focal device; two is the clip-art
  assembly. (A ground mass under the tests above is not a subject.)
- ⛔ Texture on glyphs or type, or the field texture escaping the window.
- ⛔ Real platform logos or brand marks as satellites. Generic glyphs only — a bell, a
  speech bubble, a play triangle say "social media" without trade dress.
- ⛔ A subject floating clear — nothing behind it, nothing in front.
- ⛔ Uniform-scale satellites.
- ⛔ The window as a bordered card. It is an object the story owns (a TV, a phone, a head,
  a ballot box), filled edge to edge — not a panel with content in it. If it has a keyline
  and padding, it has become a container (`06-A`). A window whose object form has rounded
  corners above 10px (a CRT screen) is `06-A`'s radius ceiling being crossed on purpose:
  name the object in the file, or it reads as a rounded card.
- ⛔ More than six satellites. Seven or more is B's territory and B's preconditions.
- ⛔ A ground mass that is halftoned, that shows a face, or that stops inside the frame.

## Build recipes (CSS, for the HTML surfaces)

- **Halftone subject:** the repeatable path is the plugin's `assets/halftone.py` (static-design,
  `assets/`): harden alpha → contrast window → duotone to the brand map → dot screen, with
  the lightest-pixel test run and the subject box printed for placement. In CSS alone:
  greyscale + contrast-lift on the cutout, then a dot screen —
  `background: radial-gradient(circle, #000 28%, transparent 30%) 0 0 / 6px 6px` over the
  image with `mix-blend-mode: screen` (light dots) or `multiply` (dark), or an SVG
  `<feImage>` pattern for print-true dots. Dot pitch 5–8px at 1080 wide; below 4px it
  aliases at feed size.
- **CRT static field:** `feTurbulence` noise over a repeating radial hue sweep,
  desaturated ~40%, inside the window's clip. Pin alpha to 1 (the `04` gotcha).
- **The window:** `clip-path` on the shape *when the shape is geometric*; an SVG mask when
  it is organic. Remember `clip-path` clips to the border box — satellites that must break
  the window edge live outside the clipped element, positioned against it.
- **The ground mass:** an `<img>` with hardened alpha, duotoned by the same script, placed
  from its measured subject box, `left`/`right` past the canvas edge on both sides.
- **Crease ground:** the four-layer ground from the handover, structure layer at low
  opacity — this is a worked surface, so no grain pass on top of the type zone.
- **A ghosted artefact set in type** is a mark, not copy: give it `aria-hidden="true"`.
  The linter then reads it as a picture and does not hold it to the text checks; a screen
  reader skips a duplicate of the hero. Without it, the device fails the safe-zone,
  register and contrast checks by construction.

The raster pipeline from `04` still applies to the subject and the mass in full: key,
harden, duotone/halftone, shared tile, torn foot or frame break.

## The measured read of the source tiles — evidence, kept separate

**The Canva tile (window build).** What it does right, in system terms: register isolation
(one halftone photograph among flat glyphs — the vox-lazy-hand mechanism); density
quarantined inside a window on a calm paper field; subject overlapped from both sides
(phone behind, bell and bubble in front); glyphs clean while the field is loud;
near-monochrome palette with the noise reading as texture; round accent full stop instead
of a typed period; type on clean ground, clear of the busy zone.

Where it breaks the system — confirmed against the files:

| Fault | Rule |
|---|---|
| Accent red on two element classes (bell badge *and* the full stop) | One accent, one element class (`03`) — pick one; the badge is the stronger carrier here |
| No source line, no base | Non-negotiable for research work (`04`, `brand.md`) |
| Everything sits inside the window — nothing breaks the window edge or the frame edge | Break the frame (`01`); the references seat their windows by letting one element cross |
| Window corner radius well above 10px | Defensible as an object form (a CRT screen), but name it in the file as the exception being used, or it reads as a rounded card |
| Figure-to-sentence ratio ≈3× | Legal only under the figure-is-the-headline reading above; if the sentence were display, it would sit outside the 1.2–2× band |

**The proof tile (ground-mass build, the 19 Aug 2026 proof tile (albo-75-tile.html, pilot folder)).** Layer order
as recorded in its header: tooth ground → ghosted `75` → bell (back) → halftoned subject
breaking the right edge → duotoned Parliament breaking both side edges and occluding the
lectern → bubble and play triangle (front) → type on clean ground. Three satellites at
176 / 132 / 56px: a 3.14× ladder, no pair within 20%. One duotone map across both assets, shadow
`#1E1E4A` to highlight `#E2491A`; the treated subject's lightest pixel measures
`(255, 80, 19)` — a colour, so the duotone happened. Contrast: ink on ground 6.73:1, muted
source line 5.16:1, navy glyphs ΔL 78 against the ground. Under `assets/check-static.py` 0.6.0 the
frame is clean once the ghost carries `aria-hidden`; the only remaining note is the
chroma-isolation warning, which the check itself says it cannot resolve on a register-
carried focal point.

**Corrected 19 Aug 2026, and worth recording as a case of the rule surviving:** the tile
first carried **two** satellites at 176 and 132 — a 1.33× ladder and a count below the
3–6 floor, so the artefact failed the spec it exists to demonstrate. The numbers above
came from the Canva tile, which had three (a phone behind the subject as well as the bell
and bubble); the build dropped it. The fix was the tile, not the ladder: a 56px play
triangle seated on the Parliament roofline, which also holds the lower-left corner the
composition was leaving empty. The scale ladder is the sticker-sheet defence and the
reference that generated the register obeyed it, so relaxing it to fit one build would
have thrown away the rule to keep the tile.

What it leaves open: the Parliament mass on disk has a soft alpha (256 values) rather than
the hardened matte the pipeline requires — it was placed as supplied and reads correctly
only because it bleeds both edges; a re-run through `halftone.py --mode duotone` from the
source cutout would settle it. And the base reads `n=[TK]` — the earlier tiles on the same
finding carry *July 2026, n=1,060*; which is right is not knowable from the files.

**Licence flags, not resolved:** the news photograph of a real public figure needs rights
checked per use; the Parliament House photograph likewise; the glyphs must be licensed or
drawn, not lifted.

## Self-check

- Exactly one photographic subject? If there is a mass, does it pass all five tests?
- Is the subject overlapped from both sides?
- Do the satellites pass the object-not-icon tests — handled, scaled, touching, no row?
- Is every texture in its own zone, and are the glyphs and type clean?
- Does the field read as one mid value at 200px?
- Is the window an object the story owns, filled edge to edge, and not a card?
- Does something break the window edge, and something the frame edge?
- Source and base above the safe line?
